UK Devolution Dashboard

Since the turn of the century, the UK has been undergoing a process of devolution; countries and regions are gaining more authority from Westminster. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland run their own health care systems, and city-regions are gaining powers too. These charts explore whether regions with more control over health look different on one measure: the share of economically inactive people who are kept out of the labour market due to long-term sickness.

Devolved nation English city-region UK 95% confidence interval

About the data

Each chart shows the share of economically inactive people aged 16 to 64 who are inactive because of long-term sickness, from the ONS Annual Population Survey (Nomis API, dataset NM_17_5). All six charts use the same vertical scale. Every area follows a similar U-shape, falling until the mid-2010s and rising since, but the levels differ: Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland sit well above the UK average, and London sits well below it. The charts show gaps between places, not proof that devolution caused them.

The data was downloaded with a Python loop, one file per area, and the six charts below are built by a single JavaScript loop. View the Google Colab notebook
